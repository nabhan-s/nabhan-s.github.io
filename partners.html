Volunteer coordination

Making it easier to lend a hand.

Helping Mamas

Helping Mamas provides diapers, period products, and baby supplies to families. Volunteers sort donations and prepare items for distribution, making their time essential to the organization’s work.

Built around the need

Our project focused on coordinating that volunteer effort. The platform’s design brings together event registration, shift calendars, volunteer records, and attendance tracking, giving organizers and volunteers a shared place to manage participation.

Why it matters

Clear sign-ups and organized records can make service easier to coordinate. For students, the project connects interface and database decisions to the people using them.

Sources: Project details · Helping Mamas

02 /

Planning & budgeting

Supporting the people behind the retreat.

Heart of Passion

Heart of Passion brings teens affected by cancer together through retreats. Behind those experiences are chapter leaders coordinating activities, donations, and budgets across many moving parts.

Built around the need

Our project brings retreat planning and finances into one interface. Its documented design includes chapter budgets, donation tracking, and an itinerary calendar, with access for both individual chapters and administrators.

Why it matters

A shared view of plans and funding can support more informed decisions. Students encounter a practical product challenge: making a tool useful for people with different responsibilities.

Source: Bits of Good’s project and partner profile

03 /

Resource navigation

Helping people find their next step.

Georgia CORE

Georgia CORE connects research, education, and advocacy to improve cancer care across Georgia. Its website serves people with different questions, including patients, survivors, and caregivers.

Built around the need

Our team developed navigation cards and user flows to help visitors locate relevant information. The project also addressed administrative tools, with the goal of reducing the burden of routine website assistance on staff.

Why it matters

Information is more useful when people can find it. This project shows students why understandable labels and thoughtful navigation matter alongside the code behind a website.

Sources: Project details · Georgia CORE

Skills with a purpose

What these partnerships make possible

We see these partnerships as a contribution to economic and social sustainability: students practice collaboration and problem-solving while nonprofits gain tools designed around their needs.

This connects to SDG 8’s focus on productive employment and decent work. Project work gives students a setting to practice skills used in the workplace. The community’s needs give that learning a purpose beyond a portfolio.
